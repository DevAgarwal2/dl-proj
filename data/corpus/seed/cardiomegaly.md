# Cardiomegaly (chest radiography)

Cardiomegaly describes an enlarged cardiac silhouette on a chest radiograph.
It is usually assessed on a frontal, upright, postero-anterior (PA) film using
the cardiothoracic ratio: the maximal transverse cardiac diameter divided by the
maximal internal thoracic diameter. A ratio greater than 0.5 is commonly taken
as abnormal in adults.

Causes include hypertensive heart disease, dilated cardiomyopathy, valvular
disease, pericardial effusion, and congenital heart disease. Apparent
cardiomegaly can also be produced by a poor inspiratory effort, an
antero-posterior (AP) projection, or patient rotation, so technique must be
considered before calling the heart enlarged.

Key radiographic features:
- Cardiothoracic ratio greater than 0.5 on a PA film
- Enlarged cardiac contour with or without pulmonary venous congestion
- Left atrial or ventricular enlargement patterns depending on the cause
