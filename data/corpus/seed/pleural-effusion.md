# Pleural effusion (chest radiography)

A pleural effusion is an abnormal accumulation of fluid in the pleural space
between the visceral and parietal pleura. On an upright chest radiograph it
classically produces blunting of the costophrenic angle and a homogeneous
opacity that obscures the hemidiaphragm, with a meniscus that is higher
laterally than medially.

Causes are often grouped by the underlying mechanism: increased hydrostatic
pressure (for example in congestive heart failure), increased capillary
permeability (infection, inflammation), decreased oncotic pressure, or impaired
lymphatic drainage (malignancy). The size, laterality, and associated features
help guide the differential diagnosis.

Key radiographic features:
- Blunted costophrenic angle (earliest sign on a PA film)
- Homogeneous dependent opacity with a meniscus
- Mediastinal shift away from a large effusion (unless there is lung collapse)
- Supine films show generalized hazy opacity rather than a meniscus
