# Pneumonia (chest radiography)

Pneumonia is an infection of the lung parenchyma that produces inflammation and
fluid filling of the alveoli. On a chest radiograph this typically appears as a
focal area of increased opacity, sometimes with air bronchograms, and it may be
lobar or patchy in distribution.

Common causes include bacterial pathogens such as Streptococcus pneumoniae,
viral infections, and, less commonly, fungal disease. Radiographic findings
should always be interpreted together with the clinical history, since
atelectasis, pulmonary edema, and malignancy can mimic pneumonia.

Key radiographic features:
- Focal or multifocal airspace opacity
- Air bronchograms within consolidation
- Silhouette sign when the opacity abuts a cardiac or diaphragmatic border
- No volume loss, which helps distinguish it from atelectasis

Reference: general radiology teaching on community-acquired pneumonia.
